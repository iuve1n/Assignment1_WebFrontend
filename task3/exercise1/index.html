Nikola Tesla

Inventor, engineer, and visionary of the electric age

About Nikola Tesla

1856–1943

Nikola Tesla was born in 1856 in Smiljan, in what is now Croatia, and died in New York City in 1943. He was a Serbian-American inventor and electrical engineer whose ideas helped shape the modern use of electricity. Tesla developed important alternating-current systems that made it practical to transmit power over long distances. His work also influenced radio technology, wireless communication, and many later electrical inventions.

Important events

  1. 1856: Nikola Tesla was born in Smiljan.
  2. 1884: He moved to the United States and briefly worked for Thomas Edison.
  3. 1887: Tesla built an induction motor using alternating current.
  4. 1893: His AC system helped power the World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago.
  5. 1895: The Niagara Falls power project began using Tesla's AC technology.
  6. 1943: Tesla died in New York City at age 86.

Key contributions

  • The alternating-current (AC) electrical system
  • The induction motor and rotating magnetic field
  • The Tesla coil for high-voltage experiments
  • Early work on radio and wireless communication
  • Remote-control technology demonstrations
